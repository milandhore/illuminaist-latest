Researchers debate whether chatbots should speak as ‘I’

MIT professor Sherry Turkle argues that first-person chatbot language can create false intimacy and blur the boundary between tool and person.

MIT professor Sherry Turkle is calling attention to the way chatbots use first-person language with people seeking companionship, therapy, or help at work. She argues that statements using “I” and “me” can make an automated system appear more intimate and trustworthy than it is.

The debate matters for product teams designing conversational interfaces, especially where users may mistake fluent language for awareness, empathy, or accountability.
