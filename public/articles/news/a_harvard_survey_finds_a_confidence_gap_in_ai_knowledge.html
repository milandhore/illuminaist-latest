A Harvard survey finds a confidence gap in AI knowledge

A survey of more than 5,400 people found that respondents often overstate what they know about artificial intelligence.

A Harvard survey of more than 5,400 Americans found that people often overstate their familiarity with AI, including by recognizing fictitious companies and executives. The results point to a confidence gap in how the public talks about fast-moving technology.

Clearer explanations and more balanced expert representation can help users make decisions based on understanding rather than confidence signals.
