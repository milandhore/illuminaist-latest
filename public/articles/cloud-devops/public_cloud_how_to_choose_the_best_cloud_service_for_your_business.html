What is cloud computing?

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services — including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, intelligence, and the Internet of Things (IoT) — over the Internet (“the cloud”) to offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale.

Types of cloud computing

There are three main types of cloud computing:

  • Infrastructure as a service (IaaS): IaaS provides the underlying infrastructure, such as servers, storage, and networking, that businesses need to run their applications.
  • Platform as a service (PaaS): PaaS provides a platform for businesses to develop, deploy, and manage their applications without having to worry about the underlying infrastructure.
  • Software as a service (SaaS): SaaS provides businesses with access to software applications over the internet.

Benefits of cloud computing

There are many benefits to using cloud computing, including:

  • Agility and scalability: Cloud computing can help businesses to be more agile and scalable. Businesses can quickly and easily spin up new resources as needed and scale down resources when they are no longer needed.
  • Cost savings: Cloud computing can help businesses to save money on IT costs. Businesses can pay for the resources they use as needed, rather than having to invest in upfront hardware and software costs.
  • Reliability and security: Cloud computing providers offer reliable and secure services. Cloud providers have a team of experts who are responsible for maintaining and securing their infrastructure.

Let's talk about Public Cloud providers.

AWS Cloud

One of the most popular and dominant cloud service providers is Amazon Web Services (AWS), a subsidiary of Amazon.com. AWS offers a wide range of cloud products and services, such as Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS). AWS has 76 availability zones spanning 245 countries and territories, and controls 32% of the cloud computing market share.

However, AWS is not the only cloud service provider in the market. There are many other competitors and alternatives that offer similar or different features and solutions. Some of the top AWS competitors are:

Azure is a cloud service of Microsoft that offers SaaS, PaaS, and IaaS. Azure is integrated with Microsoft’s ecosystem and products, such as Windows Server, SQL Server, Office 365, and more. Azure has a 19% market share and is growing faster than AWS.

Google Cloud Platform (GCP):

GCP is a cloud service of Google that offers SaaS, PaaS, and IaaS. GCP is known for its advanced AI and data tools, such as TensorFlow, BigQuery, Cloud ML Engine, and more. GCP has a 7% market share and is also growing faster than AWS.

So how do you choose the best cloud service provider for your business? There is no definitive answer to this question, as different cloud service providers have different strengths and weaknesses. The best way to decide is to evaluate your business needs and goals, compare the features and prices of different cloud service providers, and test them out before making a final decision.

Here are some factors to consider when choosing a cloud service provider:

  • Compatibility : You should choose a cloud service provider that is compatible with your existing infrastructure, applications, tools, and platforms. For example, if you are already using Microsoft products or services, you may prefer Azure over other cloud service providers.
  • Performance : You should choose a cloud service provider that can deliver high performance for your workloads and applications. You should consider factors such as availability zones, network latency, scalability options, load balancing features, and more.
  • Security : You should choose a cloud service provider that can ensure the security of your data and resources. You should consider factors such as encryption methods, compliance standards, identity and access management features, backup and recovery options, and more.
  • Cost : You should choose a cloud service provider that can offer cost-effective solutions for your budget. You should consider factors such as pricing models (pay-as-you-go or subscription-based), discounts or incentives (free trials or credits), hidden fees or charges (data transfer or support costs), and more.
