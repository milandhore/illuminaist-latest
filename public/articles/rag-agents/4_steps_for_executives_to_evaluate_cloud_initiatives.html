Summary

Driven by customer demands and investor expectations, business executives are pouring funds into public cloud initiatives to digitally transform their operations. But the bigger question that, Are these investments yielding the desired outcomes? This article empowers executive leaders with a practical four-step framework for evaluating the effectiveness of their cloud endeavors.

The overwhelming array of features and capabilities offered by public cloud providers can make it challenging for business leaders to identify the performance metrics that are most relevant to their specific needs and objectives. The hype surrounding cloud adoption often leads to the adoption of meaningless metrics that fail to capture the true impact of cloud initiatives on business operations. Additionally, the traditional view of IT as a cost center rather than an investment center, coupled with the tendency of CIOs to report performance in technical rather than business terms, hinders the evaluation of cloud initiatives’ strategic implications.

4- Step Approach

This four-step approach will help measure the impact of cloud adoption on business outcomes and assess the cloud’s value to the organization.

The first step is to focus on what matters most right now, by aligning the cloud performance measures with the business goals and priorities.

The second step is to trace where the cloud is currently affecting the business, by creating an impact map that charts the points where cloud technology is being applied to help achieve a core enterprise objective.

The third step is to translate from IT performance to business results, by correlating the cloud’s technical impact with the business performance improvements and mapping them into the standard business metrics and KPIs.

The fourth step is to measure the value not captured in financial statements, by accounting for the cloud’s strategic advantages, such as business agility, increasing returns to scale, and innovation velocity.

Step 1: Focus on Today

When assessing the value of cloud initiatives, business leaders often err by employing overly broad or high-level metrics. Since different business units within an organization evaluate value in distinct ways, aggregated metrics often prove ineffective in guiding specific business decisions.

It’s very important that what decisions you want to make based on your cloud evaluation and consider ways to narrow your requirements.

you can ask the following question before finishing with step: For example

  • Does your significant cloud computing objective include improving existing business operations?
  • Are you expecting your cloud initiatives to fuel business growth?
  • Are your goals more ambitious?

For a current assessment of your cloud investment’s return on investment (ROI), utilize lagging indicators that compare your current performance with pre-cloud metrics. Conversely, if you’re looking to anticipate future outcomes, focus on leading indicators that gauge your progress toward achieving your desired results. Ultimately, are you interested in the immediate impact of the cloud on a specific business unit, or its long-term strategic value to your organization as a whole? To evaluate direct impact, leaders should align cloud performance metrics with relevant business metrics and track changes over time. However, a strategic assessment of cloud value should focus on long-term improvements in agility, competitive advantage, and innovation capabilities.

Step 2: Track Where and How the Cloud is utilized at the Enterprise Level

Even if the long-term strategic value of cloud computing is not their primary focus, most executive leaders seek to quantify the cloud’s immediate impact on current business operations. The first step in this process is identifying the areas where cloud technology is contributing to business goals. A valuable tool for this is an impact map, which visualizes the points where cloud technology is employed to support core enterprise objectives. Start by identifying the most critical core enterprise objectives, and narrowing the analysis to the most relevant business processes, applications, and teams. Once the scope of analysis is focused on the operational areas most closely aligned with core objectives, collaborate with line leaders in these areas to determine if they are witnessing improvements attributable to cloud adoption. Executive sometimes mistakenly value the importance of cloud infrastructure based on how much use they are making of it, rather than its effect on their results.

The root cause of cloud adoption challenges often lies in two common issues:

Underutilization by the business: The available cloud services may not be fully utilized, or their usage may be inefficient or inappropriate.

Downstream process bottlenecks: Cloud-enabled benefits in one part of the value chain may fail to translate into improved business outcomes due to performance issues at subsequent stages of the chain.

As Eliyahu Goldratt aptly states in his business novel, “The Goal,” “The capacity of the plant is equal to the capacity of its bottlenecks.”

Since gaps between cloud initiatives and business results often point to cloud adoption challenges that require attention, executive leaders should not only track current successes but also identify “potential success points,” where cloud adoption is likely to enhance business outcomes if underlying issues are addressed.

Step 3: Transformation is Key

While a cloud impact map provides insights into the granular benefits of cloud adoption at the operational level, senior executives engaged in companywide investment decisions are more concerned with the broader impact of the cloud on overall business outcomes. They seek to understand how cloud initiatives are influencing key performance indicators and how future investments in cloud computing might shape overall business performance.

IT teams may already be monitoring cloud operational metrics and assessing the benefits of cloud adoption. To gain a comprehensive understanding of the cloud’s impact on business performance, executives should collaborate with IT leadership to establish “Impact Ratios.” These ratios link the cloud’s technical impact, as measured by existing IT performance metrics, to business performance outcomes identified in the cloud impact map created in Step 2.

Three key IT performance areas to focus on include:

  • Application Management : Evaluate how the cloud is enhancing the management of IT-maintained business applications. These improvements may stem from hosting applications in the cloud, integrating them with cloud services, or optimizing user access to applications through cloud-based solutions.
  • IT Service Metrics : Assess how cloud adoption is impacting crucial IT service metrics, such as time to fulfill service requests or the overall cost of service delivery.
  • Development Performance : Analyze how transitioning software development activities to the cloud has affected development performance metrics, including time to market for new versions or features, reduced rework, and lower development costs.

Step 4: Long-Term Strategic and Financial Benefit Thought Process

In addition to its immediate impact on business outcomes, senior leadership also seeks to evaluate the long-term strategic value of cloud adoption for the organization. Executives often strive to showcase this broader strategic impact by leveraging company financial statements and employing popular financial ratios that are widely understood by boards of directors and investors. How does the cloud influence key financial metrics such as gross and net margins, and return on assets (ROA). The reality is that modern financial accounting methods are not adequately equipped to capture and communicate the long-term value of cloud investments. This is because traditional financial statements do not account for the three most significant strategic advantages that stem from cloud adoption: enhanced business agility, increasing returns to scale, and accelerated innovation velocity.
