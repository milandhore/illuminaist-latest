Introduction

Sentiment analysis is the process of using natural language processing, text analysis, and statistics to analyze customer sentiment. The best businesses understand the sentiment of their customers — what people are saying, how they’re saying it, and what they mean. Customer sentiment can be found in tweets, comments, reviews, or other places where people mention your brand.

As with many other fields, advances in deep learning have brought sentiment analysis into the foreground of cutting-edge algorithms. Today we use natural language processing, statistics, and text analysis to extract, and identify the sentiment of words into positive, negative, or neutral categories.

A technical branch of computer science and engineering dwelling and also a subfield of linguistics, which leverages artificial intelligence, and which simplifies interactions between humans and computer systems, in the context of programming and processing of huge volumes of natural language data, with Python programming language providing robust mechanism to handle natural language data, and implement natural language processing easily, through its various functionalities including various libraries and functions, is termed as NLP (natural language processing) in Python.

A1: Research Question

Is it possible to use neural networks and, specifically, Natural Language Processing (NLP) to predict the positive or negative sentiment of future customer reviews based on provided labeled datasets?

A2: Objectives and Goals

The goal of this sentiment analysis is to create a model that will properly predict the positive or negative sentiment of future customer feedback based on historical, tagged customer reviews using the Python TensorFlow library. To better understand customers and make better marketing decisions, the goals also include being able to assess which products are chosen by customers based on product reviews.

A2: Prescribed Network

  • TensorFlow and Keras libraries are the deep learning neural networks that will be used in this Natural Language Processing (NLP) model.
  • Since our goal is to anticipate whether our customers prefer our items based on sentences they’ve written online as feedback for whatever they’ve bought or seen, sentiment analysis utilizing an NLP model will be up the task.

B1: Data Exploration

For analysis of opinion mining, we will be going to merge all three provided datasets from Amazon, IMDB, and Yelp into one DataFrame.

B2: Tokenization

  • The tokenization process breaks down raw text into sentences or words called tokens, which helps us comprehend the context in which we’re working. “Tokenization helps in determining the meaning of the text using sequence of words
  • We will encode the words into numbers and convert the text to a numerical representation. Scikit-learn, TensorFlow, and Keras are among the programs and tools used to normalize text throughout the tokenization process. These packages and functions are listed below in programming cells.

Please find a summary of parameters in the Tokenization process:

  • embedding dim: individual words to be embedded into 16 dimensions
  • num words or vocab size: maximum number of unique words utilized in our model
  • max length: Each review can only be 100 words long.
  • trunc type: reviews with more than 100 words are “post”-truncated (cut off at the end of the review)
  • oov tok:” Out-of-Vocabulary” (OOV) refers to the substitution of the ‘oov’ token for any unfamiliar terms.
  • padding type: short reviews will be “post”-padded (padding applied at the end of the review).

B3. Padding Process

We need a consistent sentence length for our deep learning model. The Keras padding procedure normalizes a dataset of reviews of various lengths. “Pre” and “post” padding are two options for padding. When we have a sentence length of fewer than 100 words, we have opted to pad sentences at the end, the “post” argument.

B4. Categories of Sentiment

To convert the numbers into probabilities, we applied the “softmax function. The activation function “softmax” gave us a vector as well as the probability of various outcomes.

E. Functionality

Our initial goal is to find out whether can we predict positive or negative labels based on the words our customers use in their reviews?

For these predictions, our NLP neural network was built with supervised learning in mind. Following the preparation and cleaning of this data, we constructed a four-layer Sequential model. Based on the size of this training dataset and the activation functions that we used, it appears that we cannot predict positive or negative outcomes very well after training and testing.

F. Recommendations

Again, the model’s accuracy is low, at 51.68 percent . This is disappointing and appears to send us back to the data collection phase as well as retuning our hyperparameters. We don’t believe that this NLP sentiment analysis model can be used to make meaningful recommendations. Perhaps more data from a larger customer base and a broader range of customers will provide a better understanding of customers’ mindsets or sentiments toward products and services.
