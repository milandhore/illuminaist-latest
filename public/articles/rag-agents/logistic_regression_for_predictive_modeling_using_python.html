Introduction

Predictive modeling is a method of predicting future outcomes by using data modeling. It’s one of the premier ways a business can see its path forward and make plans accordingly. While not foolproof, this method tends to have high accuracy rates, which is why it is so commonly used. In short, predictive modeling is a statistical technique using machine learning and data mining to predict and forecast likely future outcomes with the aid of historical and existing data. It works by analyzing current and historical data and projecting what it learns on a model generated to forecast likely outcomes.

B3: Appropriate Technique

  • We would like to predict Customers are at a higher risk of churn. (Response variable “Churn”) while Logistic Regression will provide probability predictions very well. We can identify that individual customer is at high risk of churn OR not.
  • Logistic regression can be used where the probabilities between two classes are required. Such as whether the customer is at a higher risk of churn or not. (Likewise True or False)
  • The logistic regression assumes that all the sample points should be independent, this helps to avoid systematic errors in data.
  • Logistic Regression won’t overfit easily. Perhaps if the degree of correlation is high enough between variables, it can cause problems when fitting and interpreting the model. Logistic regression assumes that there is no severe multicollinearity among the independent variables.
  • By adding or removing different independent variables from our regression equation, we could find out whether they have a positive or negative relationship with our dependent(target) variable. Perhaps these independent variable helps organization for decision making and improve their services.
  • Logistic regression comes with a fast and resource-friendly algorithm it scales pretty nicely.

D1: Justification of Model Reduction

  • Based on the above MLE model we created, we have a pseudo-R value = 0.4997, which is not very good for the variance of our model.
  • Coefficients on the model are very low (less than 0.5) except for variables DummyTechie, DummyContract, DummyInternetService & DummyOnlineBackup. Those variables also have p-values less than 0.000 and could be considered significant.
  • Subsequently, let us choose a p-value of 0.05 & include all variables with p-values ≤ 0.05. We will remove any predictor variable with a p-value greater than 0.05 as not statistically significant to our model.
  • We will run that reduced number of predictor variables against our DummyChurn dependent variable in another MLE model.
