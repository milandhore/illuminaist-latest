All Engineering Design Types with Examples

What & Why Agentic Approach ?

Autonomous systems Understand, plan, and execute tasks based on user instructions.
User representation Act on behalf of users by processing data, making decisions, and performing tasks independently.
Variety Range from simple single-turn assistants to complex multi-agent systems.
Capabilities Include reasoning, collaboration, and self-optimization.

Fixed Automation(No LLM)

Characteristics:

No intelligence: These systems operate based on predefined rules and do not adapt or learn from new data.
Predictable behavior: They perform tasks in a consistent and repeatable manner.
Limited scope: They are designed for specific tasks and cannot handle tasks outside their programmed capabilities.

Examples:

  • Automated Invoice Processing: Fixed automation systems can be used to process invoices by extracting data from predefined fields and entering it into accounting systems. This reduces manual data entry errors and speeds up processing times
  • Compliance Monitoring: These systems can monitor transactions for compliance with regulatory requirements by checking against predefined rules. For example, they can flag transactions that exceed certain thresholds or involve specific entities

LLM-Enhanced

Characteristics:

Context-aware: These systems can understand and respond to context within a given domain.
Adaptable within bounds: They can adapt their responses based on the context but are limited to the scope of their training data.

Finance Domain Examples:

  • AI Writing Assistants: In finance, AI writing assistants can help draft reports, emails, and other documents by understanding the context and generating relevant content. For instance, they can assist in creating quarterly financial reports by summarizing key metrics and trends
  • Chatbots: Financial institutions use chatbots to handle customer inquiries about account balances, transaction history, and loan applications. These chatbots can understand and respond to customer queries based on the context provided

ReAct — Reasoning and Action

Characteristics:

Multi-step workflows: These systems can handle complex tasks that require multiple steps.
Dynamic planning: They can adjust their plans based on new information and changing circumstances.
Fast problem-solving: They can quickly analyze situations and come up with solutions.
Access to knowledge bases: They can retrieve information from various sources to inform their actions.

Examples:

  • Project Planning Tools: In finance, ReAct systems can be used for project planning by dynamically adjusting plans based on real-time data. For example, they can optimize investment portfolios by continuously analyzing market conditions and adjusting asset allocations
  • Fraud Detection: These systems can analyze transaction patterns and detect anomalies that may indicate fraudulent activity. They can access historical transaction data and external databases to identify suspicious behavior

ReAct + RAG

Characteristics:

External knowledge access in real-time: These systems can retrieve information from external sources as needed.
Multi-tool integration: They can integrate with various tools to perform complex tasks.

Examples:

  • Legal Research Tools: In finance, ReAct + RAG systems can assist legal teams by retrieving relevant case law, regulations, and legal documents in real-time. This helps in drafting contracts and ensuring compliance with legal standards
  • Product Design Assistants: These systems can help design financial products by integrating data from market research, customer feedback, and regulatory requirements. They can dynamically adjust product features based on real-time data and insights

Multi-platform Integrator

Characteristics:

Multi-tool integration: These systems can integrate various tools and platforms to perform complex tasks.
Dynamic execution: They can execute tasks dynamically based on real-time data and changing conditions.
High automation: They automate processes to a high degree, reducing the need for human intervention.

Examples:

  • AutoGPTs for Coding Assistance: In finance, AutoGPTs can integrate multiple coding tools to assist in developing financial software. They can dynamically switch between different coding environments and libraries to optimize code for performance and compliance
  • QA Agents: Quality assurance agents can integrate various testing tools to ensure the accuracy and reliability of financial applications. They can automate testing processes, identify bugs, and suggest fixes in real-time

Memory-Enhanced

Characteristics:

Long-term memory: These systems can retain information over extended periods, allowing for more personalized interactions.
Personalization: They can tailor their responses and actions based on individual user preferences and historical data.
Adaptive learning: They continuously learn and adapt based on new information and user interactions.

Examples:

  • Personal Productivity Assistants: In finance, memory-enhanced AI can act as personal productivity assistants, helping financial professionals manage their tasks, schedules, and communications. They can remember user preferences and provide tailored recommendations
  • Project Management Tools: These tools can manage complex financial projects by retaining information about project milestones, deadlines, and team member preferences. They can adapt plans based on real-time data and historical performance

Self-Reflecting

Characteristics:

Active environment control: These systems can monitor and adjust their environment to optimize performance.
Feedback-driven automation: They continuously improve their actions based on feedback from their own performance and external sources.

Examples:

  • AutoGPTs for Smart Homes: While typically used in smart homes, self-reflecting AI can be applied in finance for optimizing office environments. For example, they can adjust lighting, temperature, and other factors to enhance productivity and comfort for financial professionals
  • Factory Automation Systems: In finance, self-reflecting AI can be used to automate and optimize back-office operations. They can monitor workflows, identify inefficiencies, and adjust processes to improve performance

Fully Autonomous

Characteristics:

Fully autonomous agents: These systems can make decisions and take actions without human intervention.
Real-time decision-making: They can analyze data and make decisions in real-time, often faster and more accurately than humans.

Examples:

  • Specialty Insurance: Fully autonomous AI can be used for real-time risk assessment and underwriting in specialty insurance. They can analyze vast amounts of data to assess risks and determine premiums without human input
  • Investment Management: Autonomous AI can manage investment portfolios by making real-time trading decisions based on market conditions and predictive analytics
